Quick Counselling Reference: SGLT2 Inhibitor & GLP-1 RA (source: KKM patient pamphlets, kemaskini Mei 2025)

SGLT2 inhibitor (Empagliflozin, Dapagliflozin, Canagliflozin, Ertugliflozin)

Take once daily, before or after food.
Benefits: ↓glucose, ↓weight, ↓BP, cardio- & renal-protective.
Side effects (low risk): UTI, genital infection, dehydration (thirst/dry mouth/fatigue).
Hygiene: clean genital area front-to-back, don't hold urine, dry thoroughly, change pads/diapers frequently.
Hydration: adequate plain water (cardiac/renal patients follow doctor's fluid advice).
Sick day rule: STOP temporarily if vomiting/diarrhoea/fever, unable to eat/drink normally, or UTI symptoms; resume once well.
Euglycaemic DKA warning signs: unusual fatigue, nausea, vomiting, severe thirst, frequent urination, fruity-smelling breath, difficulty breathing — seek care urgently.
Avoid in: allergy, end-stage renal disease/dialysis (dapagliflozin only), DKA, pregnancy/breastfeeding.

GLP-1 RA (Semaglutide/Ozempic weekly, Rybelsus oral daily, Liraglutide/Victoza daily, Dulaglutide/Trulicity weekly)

Mechanism: ↑insulin release, ↓glucagon, slows gastric emptying, ↑satiety.
Oral tablet: empty stomach on waking, swallow whole with <120mL water, wait 30min–2h before food/drink/other meds.
Injection: rotate sites to prevent lipodystrophy; fixed schedule.
Missed dose: Semaglutide inject within 5 days; Dulaglutide within 4 days; Liraglutide within 12 hours; oral Semaglutide skip & resume next day.
Storage: unopened pen in fridge (2–8°C); in-use pen at room temp away from sunlight; dispose needles/pens in sharps container.
Side effects: nausea (small frequent meals, eat slowly, ginger/mint, avoid strong odours), vomiting/diarrhoea (hydrate, avoid high-fat/fried/spicy food), constipation (fluids, fibre, activity).
Avoid in: acute pancreatitis, personal/family history of medullary thyroid cancer or MEN2, pregnancy/breastfeeding, allergy.

Lipid-Lowering Therapy Advisor — Framingham CVD risk → LDL-C target → statin suitability (tap to open)

Adapted and substantially extended from a public Framingham Risk Score calculator (itself based on the CPG Dyslipidaemia 2023 Table 1A/1B & 2A/2B FRS-General CVD tables), with this facility's own formulary and the statin-intensity table added on top so the tool goes all the way from risk score to a specific drug/dose suggestion. Sources: D'Agostino RB Sr et al., "General Cardiovascular Risk Profile for Use in Primary Care," Circulation 2008;117:743–753; CPG Management of Dyslipidaemia, 6th Ed. 2023 (Tables 1A/1B, 2A/2B, 4 & 12); 2026 ACC/AHA/AACVPR/ABC/ACPM/ADA/AGS/APhA/ASPC/NLA/PCNA Guideline on the Management of Dyslipidemia (Table 6). This is a calculation and formulary-matching aid, not a substitute for clinical judgement.

1. Framingham General CVD risk inputs

Use the average of ≥2 readings for cholesterol and BP where possible. Validated mainly for ages 30–74; ages 75–79 may be overestimated (D'Agostino 2008). HDL-C must be lower than Total Cholesterol.

2. Clinical risk-category overrides (CPG Dyslipidaemia 2023, Section 5.2.3 & Table 4) — tick any that apply

These categorical criteria take precedence over the calculated Framingham score, per the CPG: "Individuals who belong to the Very High Risk and High-Risk categories do not need to be risk stratified using the FRS-General CVD Risk Score."

2026 update — Lp(a) universal screening: every adult should have Lp(a) measured at least once in their lifetime (fasting not required; do not repeat unless clinically indicated or the assay changes) — if this has never been done and testing is available via hospital referral, consider requesting a one-off Lp(a). ≥125 nmol/L (≥50 mg/dL) is a risk-enhancing factor (tick the box above if the result is known); ≥250 nmol/L confers approximately double the cardiovascular risk. Niacin is not recommended for Lp(a)-lowering (harm outweighs benefit). CPG Dyslipidaemia 2023 does not mandate Lp(a) testing — this is additional guidance from the newer guideline.

Urine PCI (uPCR) / 24-hour urine protein → UACR A-stage converter (tap to open)

Our laboratory reports Urine PCI (urine protein-creatinine index, i.e. uPCR) but UACR has to be outsourced to Hospital Seberang Jaya, so this maps a spot PCI or a 24-hour urine protein (PER) result onto the KDIGO albuminuria (A) category, so you can still stage the patient and place them on the heat map above.

How this works — and its limits. This uses KDIGO's own published approximate equivalence between proteinuria and albuminuria categories (KDIGO CKD Guideline, "Relationship among categories of albuminuria and proteinuria" table, reproduced e.g. as Table 22.1 in Diabetes in America, 3rd Ed., NIDDK 2018): spot PCR <150 mg/g (<15 mg/mmol) or 24-hour PER <150 mg/24h ≈ A1; PCR 150–500 mg/g (15–50 mg/mmol) or PER 150–500 mg/24h ≈ A2; PCR >500 mg/g (>50 mg/mmol) or PER >500 mg/24h ≈ A3. (KDIGO's table gives spot PCR in mg/g and 24-hour PER in mg/24h the same numeric cut-points, which is why both feed this one tool.) Note these bands are not the same numbers as the ACR bands (ACR uses <30 / 30–300 / >300 mg/g) — a PCI of 200 mg/g is A2, not A2-by-the-ACR-scale, so don't type a PCI value straight into the UACR field. Important caveats: KDIGO and CPG T2DM both state ACR is the preferred measure because proteinuria includes non-albumin protein and the PCR–ACR relationship is non-linear and inconsistent at low values; CPG T2DM 6th Ed. 2020 does accept uPCR for monitoring treatment response in established proteinuria on cost-effectiveness grounds. Published numeric PCR→ACR conversion equations exist (Sumida K et al., Ann Intern Med 2020;173:426–435, developed in ~919,000 adults) but are spline-based and require sex/diabetes/hypertension terms, and the authors themselves note the relationship is unreliable below PCR 50 mg/g — so this tool deliberately gives you the category rather than inventing a single fake ACR number. If an exact UACR is needed (e.g. for a treatment threshold that hinges on ACR ≥200 or ≥300), send the sample to Hospital Seberang Jaya rather than relying on a conversion.

Adapted from the "4 Pillars of CKD Protection" sequencing summary (Dr Nixon Goyal, CKD 2024–26 Update Series), cross-checked against KDIGO 2024 CKD Guideline (Kidney Int. 2024;105[4S]), KDIGO 2022 Diabetes in CKD Guideline (102[5S]), and the KDIGO 2026 Diabetes & CKD Guideline Update (Public Review Draft, March 2026) already used for the heat map above. Currently on (tick all that apply):

Guardrails: add one pillar at a time, 2–4 weeks apart, so any side effect is attributable — recheck labs after each. RAASi: titrate to max tolerated dose; a creatinine rise up to 30% after starting/up-titrating is acceptable, don't stop; continue even when eGFR falls below 30; recheck K⁺ and creatinine 2–4 weeks after each titration; never combine ACEi+ARB+direct renin inhibitor. Benefits of the 4 pillars are largely additive (each targets a different pathway) — this is a calculation/sequencing aid, not a substitute for clinical judgement or nephrology input for complex cases.

FIB-4 (Fibrosis-4) MASLD screening calculator (tap to open)

FIB-4 risk-stratifies for advanced liver fibrosis in metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD, previously NAFLD/MAFLD) — highly prevalent in T2DM (about 49.6–72.4% in Malaysian T2DM patients, with advanced fibrosis in roughly 21%). Source: CPG Management of T2DM, 6th Ed. 2020, Appendix 9 and Tables 3-37/3-38. The CPG's Recommendation 1 (Grade A) is that all T2DM patients should have platelet count, ALT and AST performed to assess for NASH and advanced fibrosis, repeated annually or more often as indicated — so a missing or >1-year-old ALT/AST/FBC is itself a gap worth flagging.

Formula (CPG T2DM 6th Ed., Appendix 9B): FIB-4 = [age (years) × AST (U/L)] ÷ [platelet count (×10&sup9;/L) × √ALT (U/L)]. Interpretation: <1.3 = low risk for advanced fibrosis (repeat FIB-4 every 2–3 years per Table 3-37); ≥1.3 = intermediate-to-high risk — refer for liver stiffness measurement (transient elastography/FibroScan) and consider Gastroenterology/Hepatology referral. Where ALT and/or AST are elevated, also arrange an ultrasound of the liver to confirm steatosis and exclude a focal lesion, and exclude other causes (alcohol — more than two standard drinks/day for men or one for women, hepatitis B/C, and drug-induced liver injury from prescribed, OTC or traditional medicines/supplements). Caveat: a normal ALT/AST does not exclude NASH or advanced fibrosis, and FIB-4 loses accuracy at the extremes of age — it is a triage score, not a diagnosis.

CKM syndrome staging — what the stages mean

Cardiovascular-Kidney-Metabolic (CKM) syndrome staging — 2026 AHA/ACC/ADA/ASN Guideline (Ndumele CE et al., Circulation 2026;154:e50–e158). Derived automatically from the BMI, waist circumference, HbA1c, BP, triglycerides, eGFR/UACR and comorbidities already entered above.

Stage 0 — no CKM risk factors.
Stage 1 — excess or dysfunctional adiposity alone: BMI ≥23 kg/m² or waist ≥80 cm (women) / ≥90 cm (men) using the Asian-ancestry cut-offs, or prediabetic glycaemia (HbA1c 5.7–6.4%).
Stage 2 — metabolic risk factors and/or moderate-to-high-risk CKD: hypertension (SBP ≥130 or DBP ≥80, or on antihypertensives), triglycerides ≥1.7 mmol/L (150 mg/dL), metabolic syndrome, T2D (HbA1c ≥6.5%), and/or KDIGO moderate-to-high-risk CKD.
Stage 3 — subclinical CVD, or a risk equivalent: very-high-risk CKD (G4–G5 or KDIGO very high risk), or predicted 10-year CVD risk ≥20%.
Stage 4 — clinical CVD (coronary heart disease, heart failure, stroke, PAD, atrial fibrillation). 4a without kidney failure, 4b with kidney failure (eGFR <15 or on kidney replacement therapy).

Note for DMTAC: every patient here has T2D, so all are at least stage 2 by definition. The value of the staging is in catching the step up to stage 3 or 4, and in setting the right monitoring interval — stage 1 every 2–3 years, stage 2 onward yearly for lipids, glycaemia, eGFR and UACR, with anthropometrics and BP annually at every stage.

Units: the guideline is written in mg/dL; thresholds above are converted to the mmol/L this template uses (TG 150 mg/dL = 1.7 mmol/L). Subclinical-CVD markers the guideline also lists — coronary artery calcium score ≥100, NT-proBNP ≥125 pg/mL, hs-troponin — are not routinely available at this facility and are therefore not used here; a patient meeting those on hospital testing would also be stage 3.

Individualised HbA1c target

Individualised HbA1c target (KKM CPG T2DM 6th Ed. Table 3-6 / DMTAC Protocol 3rd Ed. Table 1): ≤6.5% "tight" — newly/recently diagnosed, younger, healthier, no hypoglycaemia-prone medication, low hypo risk. 6.6–7.0% — all others. 7.1–8.0% "less tight" — elderly, advanced CVD/CAD/heart failure, advanced renal failure (eGFR <45), decompensated liver disease, dementia, bed-bound, prone to/experiencing severe hypoglycaemia or hypoglycaemia unawareness, high consequence of hypoglycaemia (falls risk, drivers/machinery operators), or short life expectancy. eGFR calculation (CKD-EPI 2021)

eGFR calculation (CKD-EPI 2021, race-free): Enter Serum Creatinine, Age, and Sex above, and the calculated eGFR appears as a hint next to the "Insert calculated eGFR" button — click it to copy the value into the eGFR field (it will not overwrite anything already typed there without you clicking it). Formula: eGFR = 142 × min(Scr/κ, 1)α × max(Scr/κ, 1)-1.200 × 0.9938age × (1.012 if female), where κ = 0.7 (female) or 0.9 (male), α = -0.241 (female) or -0.302 (male), and Scr is in mg/dL (the app converts automatically if you select µmol/L). This is a calculation aid, not a substitute for a laboratory-reported eGFR when one is available.

LDL-C target by CV risk category

LDL-C target by CV risk category (CPG Dyslipidaemia 6th Ed. 2023, Table 4): Low risk (<10% 10-yr CVD risk) — <3.0 mmol/L (clinical judgement on drug therapy). Intermediate (10–20%; diabetes <50y, <10y duration, no CV risk factor) — <2.6 mmol/L. High risk (>20%; diabetes >10y duration without target organ damage plus 1 other CV risk factor; CKD eGFR 30–<60) — ≤1.8 mmol/L and ≥50% reduction from baseline. Very high risk (established CVD; diabetes with CVD/target organ damage or >3 CV risk factors; CKD eGFR <30) — ≤1.4 mmol/L and ≥50% reduction from baseline. Diabetes with proteinuria or a major risk factor (smoking/hypertension/dyslipidaemia) is classified very high risk regardless of the 10-year score.

BP target by population group

BP target by population group (CPG T2DM 6th Ed. 2020 Table 3-5/3-6 & Table 6-2; CPG Management of Hypertension 5th Ed. 2018; MDES Diabetes Education Manual): Diabetes, general — SBP 130–139 mmHg (may target <130 if tolerated in those without pre-existing coronary heart disease who are at higher risk of stroke/diabetic kidney disease, but not <120), DBP 70–79 mmHg (not <70). Diabetic kidney disease/CKD (any albuminuria level) — ≤130/80 mmHg (SBP not <120, DBP not <70). Elderly with diabetes, healthy or complex/intermediate health — <140/90 mmHg; very complex/poor health (long-term care, end-stage illness, or moderate-severe cognitive/functional impairment) — <150/90 mmHg, individualised. Source note: the CPG on Hypertension's own diabetes-specific recommendation (<140/80 mmHg, with <130/80 mmHg considered in younger/higher-risk patients) is slightly less tight than the CPG T2DM/MDES manual's 130–139/70–79 mmHg figure — both are legitimate current Malaysian guidance; this template defaults to the CPG T2DM figure as the diabetes-specific target, since that's the primary guideline for this clerking, but either wording is acceptable to document.

    Insulin titration reference — basal / premixed / bolus (tap for algorithms)
    Basal insulin

    Initiate (FPG >7.0 mmol/L, HbA1c 6.5–8.0% despite optimised OGLDs): 10 IU or 0.2 IU/kg at bedtime — 0.1 IU/kg/day if higher hypoglycaemia risk (elderly, lean, milder fasting hyperglycaemia).
    Monitor pre-breakfast (fasting) glucose. Adjust every 3–7 days once 3 consecutive readings are available:
    • >1 of 3 <4.0 mmol/L → reduce 2 IU • all 3 4.0–7.0 → maintain • >1 of 3 >7.0 without hypoglycaemia → increase 2 IU.
    Intensify (add prandial at the largest meal, 6 IU or 0.1 IU/kg) when HbA1c stays 6.5–8.0% despite FPG at goal, or once basal exceeds 0.5 IU/kg — watch for over-basalisation: basal >0.5 IU/kg/day, post-meal glucose >10 mmol/L, HbA1c off target despite FPG at target, or bedtime-to-morning difference ≥2.8 mmol/L.

    Premixed insulin

    Initiate — Once daily: 10 IU or 0.2 IU/kg, pre-dinner for human premixed, or at the biggest meal (usually evening) for analogue premixed. Twice daily: 10 IU or 0.2 IU/kg at pre-breakfast and pre-dinner; use 0.1 IU/kg if higher hypoglycaemia risk.
    Monitor — OD: pre-breakfast glucose. BD: pre-breakfast and pre-dinner. Target pre-meal 4.4–7.0 mmol/L in most patients.
    Which reading drives which dose: pre-breakfast glucose → adjust the pre-dinner dose; pre-dinner glucose → adjust the pre-breakfast dose.
    Adjust after 3 consecutive values (every 3–7 days): >1 value <4.4 → reduce 2 IU or 10–15% • all 4.4–7.0 → maintain • >1 value >7.0 without hypoglycaemia → increase 2 IU or 10–15%.
    Caution — watch for between-meal hypoglycaemia; if hypoglycaemia limits optimisation, consider switching human premixed to a premixed analogue. Continue metformin if not contraindicated; stop sulphonylurea once on twice-daily premixed.

    Bolus (prandial) insulin

    Stepwise addition — basal → add 1 bolus before the largest meal → if HbA1c still above target, add a 2nd bolus before the 2nd largest meal → then a 3rd (basal-bolus, one bolus per main meal).
    Initiate each bolus dose at 6 IU or 0.1 IU/kg per dose.
    Optimise — increase 1–2 IU or 10–15% every 3 days or twice weekly until individualised SMBG targets are met. If hypoglycaemia occurs, identify the cause; if none is found, reduce the bolus by 2–4 IU or 10–20%. Reassess treatment response every 3–6 months and intensify further if control is not achieved.
    Caution — discontinue sulphonylurea when bolus insulin is started; continue metformin.

    Source note — threshold discrepancy

    The basal <4.0 / 4.0–7.0 / >7.0 mmol/L thresholds and flat ±2 IU steps come from the MOH DMTAC Pocket Guide to Insulin Optimisation (1st Ed., Oct 2023) algorithm diagrams. The Practical Guide to Insulin Therapy in T2DM (2nd Ed., 2024) sets the band 0.4 mmol/L higher (<4.4 / 4.4–7.0 / >7.0) and allows a percentage step (±10–15%) as an alternative to ±2 IU — which is why the premixed and bolus algorithms above quote 4.4 while basal quotes 4.0. Both are legitimate Malaysian sources (the pocket guide itself cites the Practical Guide). Use local practice and document which you followed.

    5. Hypoglycaemia Assessment

    Detect by palpation/pinch test (healthy skin pinches tightly; lipohypertrophic tissue does not). Do not inject into affected areas; monitor SMBG closely and adjust dose if the injection site is changed (absorption from a non-hypertrophic site is typically faster).
